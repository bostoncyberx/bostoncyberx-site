bostoncyberx · Brand Guidelines v2.0 · August 2026 · Internal

Engineered, not decorated.

One typeface, one dark world, one red letter. This system follows the discipline of the brands we sit beside: Anthropic, OpenAI, Apple, Palo Alto Networks, and CrowdStrike all run restrained engineered sans-serif systems with a single signature accent. Nothing curly, nothing decorative, nothing that needs explaining.

01 · Logo

The wordmark is the logo.

Primary lockup: lowercase wordmark, Archivo SemiBold 640, tracking minus 12, the final x in Brand Red, with the signal line drawn beneath. The line passes behind the descender of the y, never in front of it. No accompanying symbol. The standalone red x on an ink tile is the monogram for avatars, favicons, and app icons.

Primary · the line lockup on Ink

The line lockup on light surfaces

Plain wordmark · for tight spaces where the line cannot breathe

On light surfaces the red deepens to #E02D22

Always place the lockup as artwork (SVG or PNG from the kit). Never rebuild it in type with a rule drawn separately; the line's weight and position are part of the drawing.

Monogram tile: avatars, favicon, app icon
Naked x: watermarks and pattern use only, 8 to 14% opacity
Clearspace: the height of the x on all sides.
Minimum width: 120px digital, 32mm print.
Files: true vector SVG, outlined. No font needed.

Never

Never pair the wordmark with a separate X symbol. One red x per lockup.
Never capitalize any letter, in the logo or in prose. BCX (the short form) is the only exception.
Never recolor the x to anything but Brand Red, or apply red to any other letter.
Never outline, shadow, gradient, or rotate the wordmark.
Never set the wordmark in another typeface, weight, or tracking.
Never place it on photography without an ink scrim of at least 70%.
02 · Color

Ink world. Red signature. Pillar accents.

Surfaces are ink, text is paper, and red belongs to the identity alone. The three pillar colors are functional: they mark Transform, Defend, and Achieve content, and never appear together except in the pillar story.

Red budgetRed is the signature and the emphasis: the x, the monogram, one watermark, emphasized headline words, and the defense beam. Never a button, chart color, or section wash.

03 · Typography

Archivo. One family, every surface.

A grotesque built on the American gothic tradition: flat terminals, engineered curves, made for headlines and data. One family across web, decks, print, and product keeps every surface unmistakably ours, the same discipline as Apple with SF or Google with Google Sans. Weights 400 and 500 carry text, 600 carries the wordmark and labels, 700 carries headlines.

Display 700 · minus 28 tracking
Subhead 600 · minus 20 tracking
Label 700 caps · plus 160 tracking · slate
Body 400 at 16px minimum, 1.6 line height, 70 characters maximum measure. Numerals are set in 700 when they carry an argument.

Google Fonts: Archivo 400/500/600/700/800. Desktop files: Archivo variable TTF in the kit. Fallback stack: system-ui, sans-serif. No second typeface exists in this system.

04 · Graphic language & iconography

The instrument panel, not the poster.

Blueprint grid

64px hairline grid at 35% opacity on ink surfaces. It signals engineering; never let it compete with text.

The constellation & dome

Wireframe lattice renders in pillar spectrum. Generated backdrops are right-weighted, mostly dark, under an ink scrim.

Icons

1.5px stroke, squared joins, 24px grid, slate by default, one accent when active. No filled icons, no emoji in brand surfaces.
05 · Motion

Rise, draw, snap.

PrincipleSpec

Lines drawSignal paths draw with scroll or over 600ms; never fade a line in
The x snapsLogo motion: letters cascade, the red x scales from 1.6 to 1 with a two-frame flicker, then a red rule scans out. 2.0s total, plays once.
RestraintOne orchestrated moment per surface. Everything else is state feedback under 200ms. Reduced motion: all entrances off, content visible.

07 · Voice, one card

Plainspoken expert. Calm under threat. Numbers over adjectives. Second person. Never em or en dashes. Never: cutting-edge, world-class, tailored solutions, industry leading, unlock, leverage as a verb. The name is always lowercase bostoncyberx; BCX is the short form; the x is always red.
