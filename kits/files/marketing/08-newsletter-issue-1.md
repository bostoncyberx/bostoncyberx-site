## 1. One Signal

Two numbers explain why this newsletter exists.

82% of mid-market companies have AI in production. Only 26% have it governed at scale.

Sit with that spread. It means most companies crossed the adoption line without crossing the governance line. AI is summarizing your contracts, drafting your client emails, and touching your data today, under rules that were never written.

The gap is not caused by recklessness. It is caused by structure. Companies your size rarely have a CISO, and almost none have AI leadership. So adoption is owned by whoever moves fastest, and governance is owned by nobody.

Here is the reframe we will return to in every issue: governance is not the brake on AI. It is the permission slip. The companies that write the rules are the ones that ship AI confidently, answer client questionnaires honestly, and pass insurance audits without a fire drill.

The gap is closeable. That is the work.

## 2. Threat Watch

**The pattern: AI-polished executive impersonation.** Attackers adopted AI first. The current version pairs machine-written phishing, clean and personalized, with deepfake voice or video of an executive, then pushes an urgent wire or payment change. It lands hardest at smaller firms, where one person can move money alone.

**What to do about it:** put a call-back verification rule in writing this week. Any payment change or urgent transfer request gets verified by phone, on a known number, never the number in the message. Then brief your finance team, out loud, that urgency is the tell.

## 3. Move of the Week

Run a 30-minute shadow AI check. About 55% of employees use AI tools nobody approved, so assume yours do too.

Ask five people this question, in person or by DM: "Which AI tools did you actually use this week, including personal accounts?" Promise no consequences, and mean it.

You are not hunting violators. You are mapping demand. The list you get back is your draft approved-tools list and the start of a real policy. Write the five answers down. That document is further than most companies ever get.
